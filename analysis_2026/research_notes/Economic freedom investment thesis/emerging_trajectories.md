## Q1. Latin America: Argentina, El Salvador, Chile, Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Peru, Ecuador, Paraguay, Uruguay (plus Bolivia and Venezuela)

### Takeaway
Latin America has the strongest measured shift toward markets of any region in 2025–26. Argentina posted the largest Heritage gain of any country and a top-three Fraser gain. Chile legislated a corporate-tax cut. Elections moved Colombia (June 2026), Peru (June 2026) and Bolivia (2025) to pro-market governments, and Brazil may follow in its 25 Oct 2026 runoff. Mexico moves the other way: judicial overhaul, state-first energy and falling new FDI.

**Chile (Kast, took office Mar 2026)**
- The "Reconstrucción Nacional" bill was filed on 22 Apr 2026 and passed Congress on 4 Aug 2026. Main provisions:

  - The IMF, World Bank and Chile's fiscal council warned that revenue losses outpace spending cuts. Opposition economists estimate a revenue loss of about $4.4bn/yr (about 7% of state income).
  - The opposition asked the Constitutional Court to strike the tax-stability guarantee. Later reports say the law was promulgated, but I could not confirm the date.

### Inferences
- Argentina is the clearest measured case of "more capitalism" in the EM universe: the largest Heritage gain, a top-three Fraser gain, labor reform, partial capital-account opening and record oil output. Absolute freedom levels are still low (Heritage 106th, Fraser 151st), so the trajectory is strong but the starting point is poor. The equity market already re-rated in 2024–25 (+131% in USD since Nov 2023). It is flat-to-down in 2026 while country risk rises ahead of the 2027 election. The next catalysts are MSCI (2027 at the earliest) and full removal of controls (targeted end-2026).
- Chile is the cleanest legislated tax cut (corporate 27%→23%, capital-gains repeal) in a country that already has high institutional quality (Fraser 20th). Its equity market (ECH) has not re-rated since January 2026, but the fiscal risk is real.
- Brazil is binary on the 25 Oct runoff. Current law moves toward higher taxes on capital (dividend tax, minimum tax), and a Flávio Bolsonaro win is partly priced in already (EWZ about +12% on 5 Oct).
- The 2025–26 electoral wave (Bolivia 2025, Chile, Peru, Colombia 2026, possibly Brazil) puts most large South American economies under pro-market governments for the first time in about a decade. All of them face fragile fiscal positions and fragmented congresses, so the "reform" label in Colombia and Peru is so far intent, not enacted law.
- Mexico is the main Latin American case of moving away: judicial independence weakened, energy re-nationalized, and new (non-reinvested) FDI falling despite nearshoring.

### Gaps
- No confirmation that Argentina's broad tax-simplification package (removing about 20 taxes, VAT cut) became law by Oct 2026. Exact remaining capital-control rules as of Oct 2026 are unconfirmed beyond the end-2026 target.
- Paraguay and Uruguay: no 2026-specific reform data found. Fraser 2026 ranks Uruguay 59th, and nothing else was found.
- Chile promulgation date and the Constitutional Court ruling on the tax-stability clause were not confirmed.
- Year-on-year Heritage score changes for Chile, Brazil, Mexico, Colombia, Peru and El Salvador were not retrievable. Third-party aggregator ranks conflict.
- The Colombian and Peruvian governments' enacted reforms since taking office were not found; the material is mostly campaign platforms.

## Q2. Asia: India, Vietnam, Indonesia, Philippines, Malaysia, Thailand, South Korea, Japan, Taiwan, Singapore, China

### Takeaway
Asia splits into four groups:
- **Industrialization plus a private-sector pivot (Vietnam):** about 9% growth, record FDI, FTSE EM upgrade. Heritage scores have not caught up yet (Vietnam −0.8).
- **Tax cuts plus energy build-out in a still-mostly-unfree, heavily de-rated market (India):** income-tax and GST cuts, record 55 GW of renewables, but a Heritage score of about 53, record foreign outflows and a rupee hit by the oil shock.
- **Corporate-governance capitalism with markets already re-rated (Korea, Taiwan, Japan).**
- **Moving away (Indonesia):** state capitalism via Danantara, plus market-integrity failures that triggered an MSCI downgrade consultation on 16 Oct 2026.

China is industrializing fast while remaining among the least free (Heritage 154th, Fraser 110th).

### Inferences
- **Vietnam** best fits "industrialization plus a market-ward policy pivot plus a not-yet-re-rated market". Fundamentals are near 10% GDP growth with record FDI, the FTSE upgrade has been executed, yet VNM is down about 13% YTD at about 12.5x. Heritage scores lag (−0.8), and the system remains one-party with foreign-ownership limits. MSCI EM is a 2028+ event at best.
- **India** passes the tax-cut and energy-build-out tests: income-tax and GST cuts, record 55 GW. It fails on index-measured freedom (Heritage about 53; Fraser 88th, unchanged in my data). Foreign investors are de-rating it (record $32.7bn of outflows, rupee near record lows, oil shock). It is the cheapest India has looked against EM in this sample period, but it is not a "freedom momentum" story by index measures.
- **Korea**'s reforms are governance and shareholder-return reforms, not tax or regulatory cuts. The market re-rated hard (+180% since Nov 2023) and is now in a bear-market drawdown (about −21 to −30%). The thesis would be "already priced, now volatile".
- **Indonesia** is the clearest Asian "moving away" case: state-directed capital via Danantara, market-integrity failures, and the MSCI Standalone consultation on 16 Oct 2026. A downgrade would mean forced passive selling (estimates $3bn to $19bn) through June 2027.
- **China** shows that strong industrial output (high-tech +13.8%) can coexist with very low economic freedom (Heritage 154th). It should be scored on the industrialization axis only.

Nigeria (the largest Fraser gain, FX unification, a new tax act) and Hungary (Orbán out, a two-thirds pro-EU majority) show the largest measured or institutional shifts toward market rules. ## Q5. Which countries are moving AWAY from market freedom (higher taxes, nationalization, capital controls)?

### Takeaway
The clearest movers away are:
- **Indonesia:** state-directed capital and market-integrity collapse, leading to the MSCI Standalone consultation.
- **Mexico:** judicial overhaul, energy re-nationalization and weaker regulatory independence.
- **Brazil under current law:** new dividend and minimum taxes; may reverse after 25 Oct 2026.
- **Tax raisers in Central Asia and Europe:** Kazakhstan (VAT 12→16%), Romania, Estonia, Lithuania and, by fiscal stance, Poland.

The Gulf is not moving away on policy, but the war has stalled reforms and growth. China industrializes without becoming freer.

### Inferences
- Many countries raised taxes in 2025–26 without abandoning markets. Defense spending (Baltics, Poland) and fiscal holes (Romania, Kazakhstan) are the common drivers. These should be scored "away on taxes" but not "away on property rights or openness".
- Nationalization and capital-control risk is concentrated in Indonesia (state capital via Danantara, plus index-exclusion risk) and Mexico (energy). Argentina is moving the opposite way, removing controls. Venezuela is opening its oil sector but under extraordinary, non-transparent arrangements.
- Index lag matters. Fraser 2026 uses 2024 data and Heritage 2026 uses data through mid-2025. The policy-change evidence above runs one to two years ahead of the indexes.
