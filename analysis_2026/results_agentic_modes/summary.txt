Execution modes vs buy & hold - agentic.backtest.compare_modes on public price data (no account data).
Regenerate: python3 analysis_2026/results_agentic_modes/run_modes.py  ->  modes.csv (all rows, windows "full" and "2022+").
It needs the gitignored data downloads (data/crypto_4h, data/etf), which are not in the public repo; this summary is
written by hand from modes.csv.

Modes (old MoneyLine, computed from OHLC):
  immediate = 100% on the first close beyond the line (N=1)
  split     = 50% at N=1 + 50% after 5 consecutive closes beyond the line (N=5 entries and exits)
  confirm5  = 100% at N=5
Conventions: decided at the close, traded at the next open; cost per side on every weight change; cash earns
T-bills; strategies pay 35% on each calendar year's growth (losses carried forward), buy & hold pays 20% once
at the end. trades_per_yr counts orders (weight changes): about 2 per round trip, and split places about 1.5x
as many orders as immediate, each half-size. The first 120 bars are warm-up.
Crypto: daily bars from flip_delay.load_crypto_daily, 365 bars/yr, fills 7 days a week (the spot-ETF route
waits for NYSE sessions: see gate.py). History starts 2017-12 (ETH, BTC) and 2020-12 (SOL).
ETFs: flip_delay.load_etf (dividend-adjusted closes), 252 bars/yr, from 1995 (SPY), 1999 (QQQ), 2000 (IWM).

Reading:
- Crypto, 0.15%/side: SOL and BTC favour the immediate flip (or split) over confirm5 (SOL +11.8 points after tax,
  BTC +2.8). ETH is the exception: confirm5 is 7.1 points ahead. The 21-coin basket in HANDOFF 5.15 is worse at
  every delay, so one coin is not evidence for delaying crypto. Keep immediate for crypto by default.
- Crypto, 0.95%/side (Robinhood direct): turnover is what costs (cost x |change in weight|), and split's orders
  are half-size. split beats immediate on all three (confirm5 on ETH and BTC), and on BTC every mode trails buy &
  hold after tax. Prefer the ETF route (0.02%) where it exists; on a direct-crypto vehicle consider split.
- ETFs: confirm5 > split > immediate after tax on SPY, QQQ and IWM, as in HANDOFF 5.15. But every mode trails
  buy & hold after tax by 3.0-6.5 points a year; the gain is drawdown (-30% to -49% vs -55% to -83%).
- split lands between immediate and confirm5 on after-tax return in 6 of 9 full-history rows and is above both on
  SOL (both costs) and BTC at 0.95%. It has the shallowest drawdown in 8 of 9 rows (IWM: confirm5).
- Since 2022 (modes.csv, window 2022+): every mode beats buy & hold on ETH and SOL, whose buy & hold lost money
  (-6.4% and -7.4% a year). On BTC only split and confirm5 at 0.15% edge past it (11.2 and 11.4 vs 11.1 after
  tax), and at 0.95% every mode trails it. The ETFs trail buy & hold after tax in every mode.
- Short windows and correlated coins: about two independent crypto bear markets in this data. Treat the
  differences between modes as small relative to their uncertainty.

- Reading: split is never the worst mode, has shallower drawdowns in most tests and ties or wins at Robinhood-like costs;
  the strictest test gives immediate 2-4 points a year more at low costs, within noise. Split became the crypto default.
